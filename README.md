## Can you stack a coupon on top of the annual discount?

Two third-party guides answer this differently, and neither has official documentation behind it.

One states plainly that `START10` and annual billing cannot be stacked, because the code applies to monthly plans, and advises picking one: go annual and skip the code, or stay monthly and use the code on the first month.

The other describes applying `START10` or `DATALOVER` on top of the annual discount and arrives at roughly **$39.69/month for the first year**, which is $44.10 multiplied by 0.9. That figure comes from arithmetic, not from a price anyone has reported seeing on a checkout screen. Until ScraperAPI documents the rule, the total displayed after you apply the code is the only reliable answer, and it takes about ten seconds to check before you pay.

The decision itself is simple. If you intend to run scraping for a year, take annual billing: 10% off every month beats 10% off one month, and the gap widens with every tier you move up. If you want a single month to see whether the success rate holds on your targets, pay monthly and use `START10`.

## Five limits that push people onto the wrong tier

- **Geotargeting stops at US and EU on Hobby and Startup.** Needing IPs from Asia-Pacific, Latin America or the Middle East puts you on Business at $299/month. This is the most common reason a cheap plan turns out to be the wrong plan.
- **Pay-as-you-go starts at Scaling.** On Hobby, Startup and Business, requests beyond the monthly allowance are refused with a 429 response rather than billed as overage. Your options are upgrading, contacting support, or switching on auto-upgrade so the account moves up a tier when credits run out.
- **Credits do not roll over.** Unused credits reset at each renewal, so buying a bigger plan "to bank capacity" does not work.
- **Analytics history is 30 days on Hobby and Startup.** Business and above get unlimited history, which matters if you need to compare month-over-month success rates.
- **Thread count is a hard ceiling.** 20 concurrent threads on Hobby is fine for scheduled jobs and tight for large crawls; the jump to 50 on Startup changes completion times more than the credit increase does.
